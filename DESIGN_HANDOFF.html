Website Design Handoff

Source of truth for the visual language, content architecture, and editing conventions of Chih-Yu Lin's Quarto academic homepage. Read this file before making design changes in a new conversation.

Quarto website Faculty application English content Updated 2026-08-08

1. Design intent

Academic credibility

Prioritize research identity, publications, appointments, and verifiable professional information.

Fast scanning

Faculty committees should understand the research profile, trajectory, and teaching range within one minute.

Quiet confidence

Use restrained color, generous spacing, concise copy, and light cards. Avoid decorative effects that compete with the content.

4. Page and component patterns

Homepage hero

Place a low-profile latest-updates disclosure first. Its collapsed summary shows only the newest month and milestone; expanding it reveals the complete public updates, ordered newest first and capped at five items. Dates use Mon YYYY. Every accepted paper retains its exact title, venue, and celebration symbol in the expanded content. The Visiting Researcher appointment began in Feb 2026. Move older items to the local-only .private/news-archive.html file; never publish that archive. In the two-column hero, express the two current roles as one complete sentence beneath the name. Follow with one question-led research narrative, application scope, tags, and restrained text links to Research and Contact. The right card contains the portrait only, avoiding duplicated affiliation text. Do not show expected graduation.

Research papers

Use one full-width card per public paper: verified venue/status, exact paper title, the original figure file supplied by the author, and the verbatim paper abstract. Accepted-only papers do not display a link. Formally published papers link directly to their official IEEE Xplore record. Do not add takeaway sentences or editorial bullet points.

Tags

Use pill-shaped labels for research keywords only. Do not turn normal navigation or long sentences into tags.

CV timeline

Keep the CV page unpublished until the owner supplies the finished CV. When restored, start with a direct link to the owner-supplied CV PDF or public CV URL. Then show Professional experience, followed by Education. Dates appear on the left and role/institution details on the right. Preserve reverse chronological order within each section.

Publications

Newest first within each category. Bold Chih-Yu Lin, italicize proceedings or journal names, and use en dashes in page ranges.

Calls to action

Use one primary button and restrained outline buttons. Actions should lead to research evidence, publications, CV, or profiles.

System diagrams

Use only the original figure file supplied by the author, with its original caption and descriptive alt text. Do not crop, redraw, simplify, reinterpret, merge, or add visual elements to a paper figure. When a published figure consists of multiple original files, preserve each file unchanged and arrange them with HTML/CSS only. Use a labeled placeholder until the original file is available.

Navbar

Keep Home, Research, Publications, Contact, and profile icons. Restore CV only after the owner approves the completed document. The site-owner name is intentionally hidden from the upper-left navbar because it already appears prominently in the homepage hero. The footer contains copyright only; do not repeat Google Scholar, GitHub, or LinkedIn there.

5. Content voice

  • Write in professional, direct academic English.
  • Do not invent, paraphrase, or infer research claims. Editorial titles and layout labels are allowed; technical content must come directly from the supplied paper or text approved by the site owner.
  • Use exact paper titles and verbatim paper abstracts on the Research page.
  • Use only verified publication metadata and status.
  • Do not publish submitted or under-review work unless the site owner explicitly approves that individual item.
  • Keep faculty-application framing visible but not repetitive.
  • Use official names and dates exactly as confirmed by the site owner.

6. Editing and verification workflow

  1. Read DESIGN_HANDOFF.html, _quarto.yml, and the page being changed.
  2. Preserve unrelated user changes and check repository status before editing.
  3. Keep content in .qmd files and reusable visual rules in styles.css.
  4. Preview locally with quarto preview; do not publish during drafting.
  5. Check desktop and narrow-screen layouts, navigation, external links, and publication formatting.
  6. Update this handoff file whenever a new design rule, component, or page structure is introduced.
  7. Keep the Cloudflare analytics include global through include-after-body. Do not remove the production-hostname guard or expose Cloudflare account credentials; the public site token is the only value stored in the repository.
  8. Only make the repository public and enable GitHub Pages after the owner explicitly approves launch.

Current publication state: the repository remains private and GitHub Pages is intentionally not enabled. Local preview is the review surface during drafting.
